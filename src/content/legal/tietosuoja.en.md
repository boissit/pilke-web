Pilke is an app for agreeing a date and then meeting face to face. This policy says what
personal data we process when you use Pilke, who receives it, and what rights you have.

It also concerns somebody who does not use Pilke at all: the person whose phone number a
user has saved as their safety contact.

## 2. What data we process

### Account and profile

| Email address | We contact you about your account. |

### Dates

We process the times you mark as free in the calendar, the matches offered to you and
which of them you chose, invites sent and received, agreed times and venues,
cancellations and their reasons, and the feedback given after a date.

### Date feedback

After a date we ask how it went, what you liked and didn't like about your date, what you
thought of the venue, and whether you would like to meet again. You answer by picking
options, such as personality, humour and looks, but also work, wealth and where they are in
life. There is no free text, except in safety and conduct reports.

Feedback is about both you and your date, and your date never sees your answers. For the
matching we make a summary of it, which is kept longer than the feedback itself (sections 8
and 10).

### Political views

The feedback can also include two options that reveal political views: liberal views and
conservative views. They say something both about where the person answering stands and
about how they saw their date. We process them only if you have given explicit consent
(section 5). That consent is optional and separate, and its box is not ticked in advance
when you create your profile.

- You see these options in the feedback only if you have consented.
- We store such an answer only if both the person answering and their date have
  consented. Otherwise it is dropped before anything is stored.
- In the matching, views count only between two people who have both consented.

If you did not give consent when you created your profile, you can give it later in
Settings. You can also withdraw it there at any time. We then delete every answer about
political views that you gave or that was given about you, and they stop affecting your
matches. The options are no longer shown to you.

### Whether you want to meet again

After a date we ask whether you would like to meet the person again. There are three
answers: yes and my phone number may be shared with them, yes but only through Pilke, and
no. We store your answer with that date.

If you both choose to share the number, each of you is given the other's preferred name and
phone number on that date under Past dates in the app. If you both answer yes
but either of you chose through Pilke, the two of you can be offered to each other again,
and nothing is disclosed. If either of you answers no, nothing happens and nothing is
disclosed.

We do not tell the other person what you answered. A refusal produces no notification of
any kind to either of you, so that saying no looks to the other person exactly like not
having given feedback at all.

The answer is final: it cannot be changed or taken back once you have submitted your
feedback. If you file a safety report about the other person in the same feedback, no
numbers are exchanged, even if you both asked for it. A report can only be filed in that
same feedback, not afterwards.

### Device and notifications

Sending push notifications requires a token from your device. We store the token and the
device platform. We also store which notifications we have sent and whether they arrived.

Each device you have signed in on leaves its own record, so that you can sign out of one
device at a time.

### Sign-in security

We limit how often login codes can be requested and typed, so that nobody can guess a
code or flood somebody else's number with codes. For that we process the install ID the
app creates when it is installed, and the IP address a request comes from. Of the install
ID we store only a hash. For a wrong code we also store the last three digits of the
phone number typed, and whether an account holds that number.

Of the IP address we usually store only a hash. If sign-in is blocked after too many wrong
codes, we store the address itself for as long as the block lasts, so that staff can lift
the block if needed. All of this is deleted within two days at most.

### Question sets

You answer the personality quiz when you create your profile, and its answers affect the
order your matches come in (section 10). You can also answer optional question sets, whose answers we
use for the same purpose. You answer every question set by picking an option; there is no
free text in them.

### Safety records

We process a safety report, the options chosen in it and its free text, a conduct report,
a report that the other person did not turn up, a record that you pressed the Safety button
and who the message went to, and the record that, after a safety report, the two of you are never suggested to each other again.

A conduct report is filed only if you choose to: in the feedback after a date you can tell
Pilke staff how the person you met behaved. We store who filed it and about whom, which date
it concerns, the options you chose, any free text you wrote, and how far its handling has
got. Only Pilke staff can see it.

If you say in the feedback after a date that the other person did not turn up, we store it
as a no-show report. Only Pilke staff can see it, and the other person never sees it. It
brings no restriction: a restriction follows only from a report made during the date, at the
venue (section 10).

Safety and conduct reports are the only places in the service where free text can be written
about another person. Write down only what happened.

### What we do not collect

We use no analytics and no advertising identifiers, we set no cookies on this site, and we
do not ask for your home address. The service has no chat, so there are no messages between
users. We read your device location only as described in section 3.

## 3. How we handle location

Location means two different things in Pilke.

### Your date area

You choose, on a map, which of the areas drawn in advance by Pilke you could go on a date
in. You choose them yourself, and the choice does not come from your device's positioning.
We use it to decide which venues are within reach and who we can offer you: a venue is
picked from an area you have both chosen. We store the areas you chose. Other users
cannot see your area, and you can change it in settings at any time.

### Your location at the venue

When a date is about to start, you can share your location with the person you are meeting so
that the two of you can find each other. The location is exact, and we ask for permission
separately.

Sharing is limited in five ways:

1. The date has been agreed and has a venue.
2. The window is {{sharing.window_minutes}} minutes: from {{sharing.lead_minutes}} minutes before the agreed start to
   {{sharing.trail_minutes}} minutes after it.
3. You are within {{sharing.radius_m}} metres of the venue. Your device checks the distance before it sends
   anything, and the server checks it again.
4. Only the person you are meeting can see it. No other user can, and it does not appear in
   our admin views.
5. The location is deleted when the window closes. If you stop sharing yourself, it is
   deleted immediately and no record of it is kept.

Sharing continues if you leave the app or put the phone in your pocket. We say so in the
app as well, when we ask for permission. The location is not used for anything other than
helping the two of you find each other.

## 4. What other users see about you

Another user sees your preferred name, age, gender and photograph. They also see the agreed time
and venue, the times you have offered them, and your location at the venue if you share
it.

Another user does not see your email address, your date of birth, your safety contact's
number, your date area, your preferences, any restriction placed on you, or the feedback
you give or that is given about you.

They do not see your phone number either, with one exception: if the two of you have both
chosen, after the date, that the number may be shared, you are each given the other's
preferred name and number. If only one of you chooses that, or neither does, your number is told
to nobody. Section 2 says how that question works.

## 9. When you delete your account

You can delete your account in the app's settings. It happens immediately and cannot be
undone. There is more detail on [How to delete your data](/en/tietojen-poisto).

We delete your phone number, email address, preferred name, date of birth, gender, photograph,
safety contact's number, date preferences, date area, calendar entries and answers to
question sets, and your matching profile. The photograph is deleted from storage as well,
and every device is signed out at the same moment.

The summaries made from feedback are handled like this:

- **Summaries of feedback given about you** are deleted at once, and no longer affect
  anybody's matches. You are also removed from the comparisons in which other people were
  compared with you.
- **Summaries of feedback you gave about others** stay, anonymised, because they are also
  your dates' data and their accounts still exist. Everything that says anything about
  you is removed: who gave the feedback, what it said about what you value, and which
  feedback it came from. All that is left is how your answers about your date differed
  from your usual answers, for example that somebody liked their humour more than usual.
  Nothing links it to you.

Two groups remain:

- **Data that belongs as much to the other person.** Past dates, the feedback given about
  them, and no-show reports. The other person sees *Somebody* where you used to be.
- **Safety decisions that leaving must not undo.** Safety and conduct reports, and the
  record that, after a safety report, the two of you are never suggested to each other
  again.

Both are kept for the period set out in section 8, and your name is no longer attached to
them. A record that the account existed also remains, so that we do not create two
accounts for the same number. You can register again with the same number; you get an
empty account, and nothing links it to the old one.

## 10. How we make automated decisions

Finding matches is automated, but it only produces a suggestion. You decide who to
invite, and you can pass on every match.

Putting matches in order is profiling: from your data we assess automatically who might
suit you and whom you might suit. The order depends on

- **your personality quiz answers:** at first we suggest people whose answers go well with
  yours;
- **your own dates:** your feedback teaches us what kind of people suit you in particular,
  and after just a few dates it counts for more than the quiz;
- **everyone's dates:** a model learns from all users' feedback which quiz answers go well
  together, so even a new user benefits from it straight away. The model itself holds
  nothing about any individual;
- **feedback given about you:** the impression you have made on your dates, for example
  through your behaviour or your humour;
- **similar tastes:** if people who liked the same things in somebody as you did also
  liked them in somebody else, we are more likely to suggest that other person to you;
- **how often someone has been suggested:** somebody you were shown recently and did not
  invite moves down, and somebody being suggested to a great many people right now moves
  down a little, so that the same people are not shown to everyone.

Feedback works in both directions: on whom we suggest to you, and on whom we suggest you
to. There is also an element of chance in the order, so that the same people are not
always at the top.

Profiling changes only the order. It excludes nobody from the suggestions, and it has no
legal or similarly significant effect on you, because you decide who to invite.

For every suggestion we record a breakdown of how the people in it were scored: a number
for each of the parts listed above, and their total. It covers only the people shown, and
it does not contain the feedback itself. We need it to work out afterwards why a
suggestion came out as it did, and to improve the ordering. It is kept for the life of your
account and deleted with it (section 8).

Restrictions, which the app calls time-outs, also arise automatically, from cancelling an upcoming date or not turning up. A
restriction means you are not shown among other people's matches, or you cannot look for new
matches, and it lasts no more than 30 days.

One restriction rests on another user's report: if your date reports that
you did not turn up, the restriction follows automatically. You can file your own report
within 24 hours, which lifts it. You can also ask for the decision to be looked at again by
writing to the address in section 1, and a person will then review it.

A safety report never triggers a restriction automatically. A conduct report or a no-show
reported in the feedback brings no restriction.

If two different users say in their feedback within 90 days that the same person did not
turn up, or that their photos gave a different impression, Pilke staff are told. A person
looks into it, and the alert by itself leads to nothing automatically.

## 11. Your rights

You have the right to

- access your data and receive a copy of it;
- have inaccurate data corrected;
- have data deleted, to the extent the law allows (section 9);
- restrict processing, and object to it where the basis is legitimate interest;
- receive the data you gave us in a portable form;
- withdraw consent, where the processing rests on it;
- not be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing (section 10).

Some of this you do yourself: you change your profile and preferences in settings, you stop
sharing your location with one tap, and you delete your account in settings. Send other
requests to the address in section 1. We will confirm that the request comes from you and
answer within one month. If the request is extensive we may extend that by two months, and
we will tell you if we do.

Withdrawing consent affects processing from then on. You stop sharing your location with
one tap, and you can change your date preferences at any time. You can give and withdraw
consent to political views in Settings, and withdrawing deletes every answer about them that you gave or that
was given about you. The answer about meeting
again cannot be withdrawn: once the numbers have been exchanged, withdrawing does not get
them back. Section 5 says what withdrawing means in that situation.

If another user has filed a safety or conduct report about you, you cannot see what it says. Otherwise
the person who filed it would be identifiable and reporting would become impossible. You
can still ask us to confirm whether we hold such data about you.

Date feedback needs the same balance. Feedback about you is your data as well, but your
dates have been promised that you will not see their answers. On request we tell you what
feedback has been given about you, as a summary across all your dates. We do not give
individual answers, who answered what, or which date the feedback is from. If there is
feedback from fewer than three dates, a summary would reveal somebody's answers, so we
then only tell you that there is feedback and what it is used for. Feedback you gave
yourself you get in full. Send your request to **contact@pilke.app**.

Using date feedback in the matching rests on the contract, so the right to object does not
apply to it. You can still write to us if you suspect feedback about you was given out of
spite or is otherwise misuse, and we will look into it. Sign-in security rests on
legitimate interest, so you can object to that.

## 12. How we protect your data

The account has no password: you identify yourself with your phone number and a one-time
code, and each device signs in separately. We store only a hash of the session token, and on
the phone the token is held in the operating system's secure store. Requesting and typing
login codes is limited, so that a code cannot be guessed (section 2).

Connections between the app and the server are encrypted. Admin access is limited to those
who need it for their work, and shared locations do not appear in the admin view at all.

If a change affects what is done with your data, we will tell you in the app before it takes
