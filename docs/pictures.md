# Which picture is on which page

Every screenshot on this site is a still of a real build, so a change to the app's UI can
make any of them a picture of something the product no longer does. This document exists so
that finding out *which* ones is a lookup rather than a hunt through components: the mistake
it is here to prevent is reshooting the screens you happened to think of and shipping the one
you forgot.

Two tables. The first is generated from the build and lists what is drawn where. The second
is written by hand and says what each picture shows, because that is the part a script cannot
read off a file.

## When the app changes

Every picture is regenerated by two commands, one per repository, and a picture a
human has to make by hand is a bug in them. Regenerate all of them rather than the
ones you think the change touched: that guess is how a page ships a stale screen.

1. **Capture**, in `pilke-app`, about 100 minutes:

       scripts/capture-site-pictures.sh

   It resets the local e2e backend, runs every flow the site needs in both
   languages (the marketing screens, the manual's stills, the busy calendar week,
   the upcoming date), writes each still straight to its place in this repository,
   puts the database and every account it touched back, and ends with a summary
   that fails if any file is missing, was not rewritten, or is not 1080 x 2280.
   It also fails on a manual sidecar missing a callout the contract asks for: a
   date's first minutes are timed, and a slow run can photograph the next state.
   `--list` shows what each step writes; `--only <step>` re-runs one, and
   `--set <name>` one of the marketing sets inside it.

   It needs the release APK built against the local backend on the
   `Pixel_4_API_30` emulator (`pilke-app/docs/e2e.md`, "The short version"), and
   refuses anything else: a development build lays LogBox toasts over the stills,
   and the `preview` build (`npm run build:ci`) is baked against production.

2. **Build**, here, after `npm ci`, about 3 minutes:

       npm run pictures:build

   It makes a pinned Python (`scripts/photos/requirements.txt`, in
   `scripts/photos/.venv`), fetches the Unsplash originals named in
   `scripts/photos/originals.json` into `scripts/photos/full/` and checks their
   hashes, checks every still against the capture profile, makes every photograph
   and crop in both languages, builds the site, rewrites the inventory below and
   runs `npm run pictures -- --check`. The same stills give the same bytes: run
   twice, it changes nothing the second time.

3. **Review the diff.** `git status` lists every picture that changed; look at each
   one, and at these in particular, which a script cannot judge:
   - `hero-cutout-*` and `hero-cutout-phone.png`: the hand and phone cut out of the
     photograph. The cut is fitted on the Finnish composite, and the mask decides
     what stands over the coral disc's edge. On the front page at 1440 and 390,
     the phone's outline and the fingers over it should be clean, with no room
     showing round them.
   - `beta-invitation-*`: a thumb lies over the lower screen.
     If the app's layout moved, it may now cover something it should not.
   - The phone frames on `/` and `/en`: each in its page's language, the dates
     ahead of today, and no *Nämä treffit ovat jo olleet* under the date.
   - The marketing stills' status bars: SystemUI's demo mode, so only `9:41`, full
     signal and a full battery. A notification icon there means the capture ran
     without it, and the phone frames, the hero and the beta card all show the bar.
   - The bottom of every phone frame, at 1280 and at 390: the app's own button row
     or tab bar with its gutter under it, nothing cut by the rounded corners, and no
     band of empty screen. A screen that has moved its controls nearer a corner, or
     a capture moment that leaves the screen mostly empty, shows here first.

4. **Commit** the stills, the pictures and `docs/pictures.md` together, on a branch.
   `main` deploys to pilke.app on every push.

**What stops the build instead of making a wrong picture.** Every composite and
the phone frame cut or keep the Android status bar (the top 96 rows) and navigation
bar (the bottom 136) at fixed rows, measured on the Pixel 4 profile. `scripts/photos/stills.py`
checks every still for that profile's size, its three navigation buttons and its
clock where those rows put them, and that `Phone.astro` crops the same rows and
draws its camera in the bar it keeps; a still from another device, gesture
navigation, or an app drawing into a bar fails by name.
An original that changed upstream fails its hash, since every screen corner is fitted
on the recorded file, and `cutout.py` checks that its copy of the hero's fitted screen
still matches `hero.py`'s.

**What a reader sees of a marketing shot is the file less its navigation bar.**
`Phone.astro` crops the three-button bar off every picture in `src/assets/screens` and
draws the rest edge to edge, the status bar included, under a punch-hole camera; the
app's own button rows leave the gutter the rounded corners need. Nothing is inset on a
margin of the app's colour, so no screen needs one recorded for it. The manual's stills
are drawn whole, bars included, in an ink bezel.

Not every screen the flows take is carried here. `screenshots.yaml` also shoots the
calendar and the question sets, and neither has a place on the site; the way to bring
one back is to add it to `MARKETING` in `pilke-app/scripts/capture-site-pictures.sh`
and give it a row below.

## The photographs

Two pictures on the front page are stock photos from Unsplash with a still set into
the phone in them, each made once per language so that `/en` shows the English app. A
still in a photograph goes stale with the app like any other, and more quietly, because
nobody reads a photograph for its UI. The stills the photographs alone use are kept in
`scripts/photos/stills/`; the rest are read from `src/assets/screens/` and
`src/assets/manual/`, never copied. `npm run pictures:build` makes all of them, from
the originals `scripts/photos/originals.json` records by URL, size and SHA-256. The
screen corners are fitted once on those files and fixed in each script, which is why
a changed original stops the build.

face (the beta card's crop leaves out the right fifth of its original, where a
blurred cheek is), and none may be captioned as a Pilke user.

## The sparks

The sparks in the price lines on `nain-se-toimii` are not pictures and not
screenshots: `Sparkle.astro` draws them as vector SVG from `sparkleArtwork.ts`, whose path
is the wordmark's own sparkle and whose colours and core steps are the app's counter's. So
they cannot go stale against the app's mark the way a render could, and they need no file
per amount. The sparkles scattered behind the cards are the same path, drawn flat by
`SparkleScatter.astro`.
